M2 · PEER CASE STUDY · JEJU
M2-03-2
Jeju's lesson: rolling devolution works, but each round lagged about 19 months — so the island now demands one comprehensive transfer.
What worked, what failed, and what transfers to Aceh, 2006–2026.
WHAT WORKED
Seven scheduled rounds moved 4,660 powers — devolution treated as a process, not an event.
Investment Promotion Zones and visa-free entry gave the new powers a commercial use.
Per-capita output roughly doubled to $31,150; 13.0m visitors in 2024.
WHAT FAILED
About 19 months of lag per round while the National Assembly processed each amendment.
Every batch re-negotiated from scratch; there was never an automatic pipeline.
Jeju now lobbies for one "comprehensive transfer" to escape the round-by-round model.
ACEH TRANSFER
WHAT ACEH COPIES
A standing devolution commission with a published schedule and a power-by-power list.
Time-boxed rounds tied to the RUU UUPA, and a zone-level tax offer at KEK Arun and Sabang.
WHAT ACEH CANNOT COPY
Visa-free entry decided by the province — immigration is a national power in Indonesia.
An autonomous police force — outside the Helsinki settlement and the UUPA.
INSTRUMENT NEEDED IN INDONESIA
Perpres for a joint Aceh–Jakarta devolution commission under UUPA Ps 8 and Perpres 75/2008.
The nine missing PPs listed with dates inside the RUU; a KEK fiscal package for Arun and Sabang.
SO WHAT
Take the conveyor, skip the lag: put the missing PPs on a dated schedule inside the RUU so Aceh does not wait 19 months per batch, as Jeju did.
