M2 · PEER CASE STUDY · BASQUE COUNTRY
M2-02-2
Basque lesson: collecting your own taxes works, but tax competition is policed — the 1995–2000 holidays were struck down as illegal state aid.
What worked, what failed, and what transfers to Aceh, 2002–2026.
WHAT WORKED
Regional collection of the four big taxes — €18.31bn in 2024 — by professional provincial treasuries.
A five-year cupo law (Law 10/2023) that removes annual bargaining over the quota.
The region keeps about 92% of what it collects and pays Madrid a defined €1.53bn.
WHAT FAILED
The 1995–2000 corporate tax holidays were ruled illegal state aid by the EU Court of Justice in 2011.
The cupo method is re-contested at each five-year renewal.
The model rests on a constitutional anchor Indonesia's 1945 Constitution does not provide.
ACEH TRANSFER
WHAT ACEH COPIES
An assigned-tax schedule with a five-year settlement law, so the fiscal deal is not re-opened every budget.
Own administration for the assigned taxes, with zakat-as-tax-credit as the first instrument.
WHAT ACEH CANNOT COPY
Full VAT and corporate-tax collection — both are centralised under Indonesian law and outside the UUPA.
An external referee on tax competition; in Indonesia the referee is Kemenkeu itself.
INSTRUMENT NEEDED IN INDONESIA
RUU UUPA tax-share clause (at least 70/30) and the Ps 166 fiscal-facilities PP that has never been issued.
PP on zakat as an income-tax credit (Ps 192), stalled for 20 years; DBH formula published with the calculation.
SO WHAT
Phrase the fiscal ask as assignment, not aid: name the taxes, name the settlement period, accept a referee. That is what survived in the Basque case; the tax holidays did not.
Source: EITB, EU Court of Justice on Basque tax holidays, 2011; BOE, Ley 10/2023; Concierto Económico overview. [910][908][555]
