M2 · PEER CASE STUDY · SARAWAK
M2-07-2
Sarawak shows Aceh the play: use every ordinance-level power first, then litigate and negotiate from a position of revenue.
Sarawak lessons mapped to Aceh's qanun, BPMA and UUPA Ps 160–161 toolkit, with the Indonesian instrument each needs.
WHAT WORKED
Ordinance first, litigation second: the state legislated a 5% sales tax, then sued Petronas to collect it. RM19.55bn followed.
Company before mandate: Petros existed and operated before Putrajaya conceded the aggregator role on 1 Mar 2025.
Constitutional recognition (Art 1(2), Dec 2021) came last, after revenue facts on the ground made it cheap to grant.
WHAT FAILED OR STAYED OUT OF REACH
MA63 said nothing about oil, so more than five decades of rent flowed out before the state found a tax handle in 2019.
Upstream ownership is still federal. Sarawak's leverage is fiscal and downstream, not title to the resource.
SO WHAT
Aceh already holds what Sarawak litigated for: a UU with a 70% split and joint management. Use qanun, BPMA and PEMA the way Sarawak used ordinances and Petros; litigate Ps 172–173 and the 12-mile cap the way Sarawak litigated its tax.

Copies now

Litigation as a lever (SST suit)
Copies now

Own tax on the resource flow (5% SST)
Needs Jakarta
UUPA allows local taxes only within national law; the RUU's tax share (≥70/30) and a PP under Ps 166 are the nearest instruments
Own immigration (Part VII)
Cannot copy
Immigration is national in a unitary state; the realistic ask is a talent-visa and port-of-entry sandbox for Sabang and Arun by Perpres
Constitutional restatement (Art 1(2))
Partial analogue
UUD 1945 Ps 18B already recognises kekhususan; the working equivalent is restoring MoU 1.1.2 consent in the RUU UUPA (Ps 8), not a constitutional amendment
