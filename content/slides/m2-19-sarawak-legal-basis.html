M2 · PEER CASE STUDY · SARAWAK
M2-07-0
Sarawak's 1963 treaty has no oil clause, so the state built its leverage through its own ordinances and litigation.
Malaysia Agreement 1963, the Dec 2021 Art 1(2) amendment, and the state instruments Sarawak used, 1963–2025.
LEGAL INSTRUMENTS, FEDERAL AND STATE
1963
Malaysia Agreement 1963 (MA63)
Sarawak joins with safeguards on immigration, land and religion. The treaty is silent on oil and gas.
1963
Immigration Act, Part VII
The state controls entry of non-residents, including other Malaysians (Section 66 exemption endorsement).
2016
Distribution of Gas Ordinance (amended 2023)
State ordinance licensing gas distribution; the legal base for Petros as gas aggregator from 1 Mar 2025.
2019
5% state sales tax on petroleum products
Levied under state sales-tax powers; contested in court by Petronas; RM19.55bn collected by end-2024.
2021
Federal Constitution Art 1(2) amendment, Dec 2021
Restores Sabah and Sarawak as partners of the Federation, distinct from the Malayan states.
WHO HOLDS THE POWER
Immigration
State; entry permits for all non-residents
Land, forestry, customary rights
State list under the federal constitution
Sales tax on petroleum
State ordinance, 5% since 2019; defended in court
Oil and gas
Federal law vests upstream; state answers with gas ordinance + Petros
Courts, money, borrowing
Federal; no separate judiciary or sovereign credit
SO WHAT
Sarawak had no oil clause; Aceh has Ps 160–161 and a 70% split written into a UU. Aceh's paper position is stronger, Sarawak's execution is stronger. The gap is instruments used, not rights held.
