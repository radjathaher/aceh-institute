M2 · PEER CASE STUDY · SHENZHEN SEZ
M2-05-2
Shenzhen's lesson: empowerment was selective and revocable — it lasted because the zone kept delivering results the centre wanted.
What worked, what limits it, and what transfers to Aceh, 1980–2026.
WHAT WORKED
The 1992 legislative authorisation let the zone write its own economic rules.
A list-based reform pilot — 40 named items in 2020 — rather than open-ended autonomy.
A port plus a manufacturing hinterland: output up roughly 13,600 times since 1980.
WHAT LIMITS IT
Empowerment is selective and revocable — the centre keeps the pen at every stage.
The "Shenzhen exception" was never generalised to other cities (Song and Tan, 2025).
No fiscal formula: results, not entitlement, kept the powers in place.
ACEH TRANSFER
WHAT ACEH COPIES
A named authorisation list with deadlines and results-based renewal, not open-ended autonomy.
Port-and-hinterland framing for the Arun–Lhokseumawe corridor: gas, land, jetty, and one operator.
WHAT ACEH CANNOT COPY
Zone regulations that override national law — a qanun cannot; the variance must be granted in a PP.
Revocation at the centre's discretion without process; Aceh needs the consent rules Shenzhen never had.
INSTRUMENT NEEDED IN INDONESIA
RUU UUPA lex specialis clause with a working consent procedure under Ps 8.
PP list for KEK Arun and Sabang with a five-year review; a Perpres time-boxed pilot on ports, telecom licensing and land.
SO WHAT
Ask for a Shenzhen-style list, but attach Helsinki-style consent: the list gives Jakarta control, the consent clause stops the list being revoked by silence.
Source: Song and Tan, The Shenzhen exception, Journal of Current Chinese Affairs 2025; AsianLII, NPCSC 1992; State Council 2020. [924][921][922]
