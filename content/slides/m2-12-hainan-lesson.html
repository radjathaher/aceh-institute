M2 · PEER CASE STUDY · HAINAN FTP
M2-04-2
Hainan's lesson: a free port is a customs perimeter plus rules of origin — the 30% value-added test decides who actually benefits.
What worked, what binds, and what transfers to Aceh, 2021–2026.
WHAT WORKED
One customs line around the whole island, live since 18 Dec 2025.
Zero tariff on 74% of lines and 15% income-tax caps for qualifying firms and talent.
Actual FDI of ¥25.1bn in 2025, up 19.9% in the closure year.
WHAT BINDS
The 30% value-added rule blocks pass-through trade into the mainland; only processed goods win.
Second-line controls add compliance cost to every shipment leaving the island.
Only the centre can legislate customs and tax; the province implements what Beijing writes.
ACEH TRANSFER
WHAT ACEH COPIES
A bonded perimeter for Sabang and the Arun–Lhokseumawe corridor, with bonded movement between the two.
Rules of origin with a value-added test, so the zone rewards processing rather than transit.
WHAT ACEH CANNOT COPY
Province-set tariffs and income-tax caps — both sit with Kemenkeu and the DPR.
An island-wide closure without a national customs decree; Sabang's line must be drawn in Jakarta.
INSTRUMENT NEEDED IN INDONESIA
PP under the KEK law and the Sabang free-port law, reviving the Dewan Kawasan; a Kemenkeu customs rule for the corridor.
A BPKS reset: Rp4.83T of state funding in 2003–24 returned Rp48.7B in PNBP.
SO WHAT
Hainan proves the perimeter works only with rules of origin and a live customs authority. Ask for the line and the rule together, and rebuild BPKS before asking for more land.
